Style Guide

How to choose glasses for your face shape (and when to break the rules).

Round face, angular frames. Square face, soft curves. The face-shape rules exist because contrast flatters — but they're a starting point, not a law. Here's how we actually fit frames.

The rules, in one honest paragraph

Opposites flatter: round faces gain structure from angular frames; square jaws soften behind curves; heart-shaped faces balance with bottom-heavy or rimless styles; oval faces wear nearly everything. Scale matters too — a frame should roughly match the width of your face at the temples.

That paragraph is 80% of every face-shape article ever written. It's useful. It's also incomplete.

What the charts miss

Coloring, brow line, hair, personality, and how you actually live matter as much as geometry. A frame that follows your brow line looks intentional. A color that echoes your eyes or hair reads as harmony. And the frame a confident person loves beats the frame a chart approved.

This is why we fit in person: five minutes of watching which frames make you stand differently in the mirror tells us more than any quiz.

Our fitting method

We pull three families: one safe, one interesting, one you'd never have picked. You'd be amazed how often the third one wins. Then we dial fit — bridge, temple length, pantoscopic tilt — because comfort is what makes you actually wear them.

Come play. The cases at Eyeland Optique hold everything from invisible Danish titanium to Parisian statement acetate, and trying them on costs nothing.

Quick answers

Common questions.

What glasses suit a round face?

Generally, angular and rectangular frames add definition to softer features — but proportion matters more than shape. A slightly wider frame with a strong browline does more than any rulebook.

What frames make you look younger?

Frames slightly bolder than you think, honestly. A crisp acetate with some color lifts the face; tiny wire rectangles age almost everyone. Let an optician push you one notch braver than your instinct.

How should glasses fit properly?

Frames should sit level, hug (not pinch) the temples, leave your eyes centered in the lenses, and not slide when you look down. If you're constantly pushing glasses up your nose, they were never fitted — come in, adjustments are free.
