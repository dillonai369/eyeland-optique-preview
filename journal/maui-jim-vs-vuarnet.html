Sun & Sport

Maui Jim or Vuarnet — which polarized lens for which water?

Living on an island means glare is a daily fact — off the Sound, off the sand, off the hood of the car on 278. We carry two of the most serious polarized-lens makers in the world, and they're not interchangeable. Here's how we think about the choice.

Maui Jim: the all-day island lens

Maui Jim's PolarizedPlus2 blocks 99.9% of glare and actively boosts color saturation — greens get greener, blues get deeper, and the marsh at golden hour looks the way it should. It's the most versatile lens we carry: boat mornings, Harbour Town afternoons, dock dinners.

If you're going to own one serious pair of polarized sunglasses on Hilton Head, this is our default recommendation — and we carry the full prescription line.

Vuarnet: mineral glass, French soul

Vuarnet has made its lenses from mineral glass — never plastic — in its own Alpine factory since 1957. Glass is optically clearer, dramatically more scratch-resistant, and ages better than any polymer lens. The Skilynx tint was built for high-altitude snow glare, which makes it shockingly good on open water too.

Vuarnet is the choice when style matters as much as function: the frames Steve McQueen wore, with lenses that outlast the trend cycle.

The honest answer

Most of our regulars end up with a favorite of each: a Maui Jim that lives in the beach bag and a Vuarnet that dresses up dinner at the harbour. Come in and try both against the parking-lot glare — five minutes outside tells you more than any article.

Quick answers

Common questions.

Which is better for boating — Maui Jim or Vuarnet?

For all-day boating most of our clients choose Maui Jim for its PolarizedPlus2 color boost and lighter weight. Vuarnet's mineral glass wins on optical purity and scratch resistance if you don't mind a little more weight.

Can I get either brand in my prescription?

Yes — we fit prescription sun lenses in both, including progressives. Bring your current prescription or book an exam and we'll build them from scratch.

Are glass or plastic sun lenses better?

Mineral glass (Vuarnet) is optically clearer and nearly scratch-proof but heavier. Modern polarized plastics (Maui Jim) are lighter and safer for sport. The right answer depends on how you'll wear them — come talk it through.
